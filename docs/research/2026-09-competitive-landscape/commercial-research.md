# Commercial / closed-source macOS voice dictation apps — competitive research

Research date: **2026-09-11**. Method: first-party sources only wherever obtainable (product sites, vendor docs, help centers, privacy/security pages, Apple support and developer docs, Apple's own iTunes Search API). SEO comparison blogs (spokenly.app, getvoibe.com, lumevoice.com, heymumble.com, weesperneonflow.ai, laxis.com) were used only as leads and are never cited as fact. Anything unverified is flagged explicitly rather than guessed.

All App Store star ratings, rating counts, versions and release dates below were pulled live from Apple's iTunes Search API on 2026-09-11, so they are hard numbers rather than blog claims.

## 2. Per-app detail

### Superwhisper — the local-first platform play

- **Company**: SuperUltra Inc, Toronto. Founder Neil Chudleigh, 32. Launched July 2023. Six employees plus five contractors as of May 2026. **Bootstrapped, no outside investment.**
- **Traction**: hundreds of thousands of weekly active users; revenue described as seven figures and growing quickly. Named customers: Meta Platforms, OpenAI, Coinbase, Dropbox, plus US financial institutions. iOS App Store: 4.38 stars, 822 ratings, v2.23, updated 2026-09-09.
- **Pricing**: Free tier; Pro $8.49/month, $84.99/year, **$249.99 one-time lifetime**. New users get 3,000 words of full Pro access as a trial (this replaced an older 15-minute trial in Aug 2026). One licence covers unlimited Macs and Windows PCs plus iPhone and iPad. 40% student discount. Enterprise tier adds SOC 2 Type II, team billing/auth, model access controls, enterprise-hosted models.
- **Documented Free vs Pro gating**: cloud voice models limited on Free vs unlimited on Pro; local voice models, custom modes, AI-powered modes, custom vocabulary, speaker separation and priority support are all Pro.
- **⚠️ Pricing conflict worth acting on**: the plans doc gates *local voice models* behind Pro, but changelog entry **2.18.2 (Aug 26 2026)** states "All local Whisper models now free on macOS." These two first-party sources disagree. If the changelog is current, Superwhisper now gives away exactly the capability a local-first competitor would charge for on macOS.
- **Local STT models** (all on-device): Parakeet V2 and Parakeet V3 (NVIDIA), Ultra V3 (= OpenAI Whisper **large-v3**), Ultra V3 Turbo (= large-v3-turbo), Standard, Pro, Nano, Fast (Whisper variants, some English-only), plus Cohere Transcribe. Local models are Apple-Silicon-oriented; the vendor's own support notes say Intel Macs are better served by cloud models.
- **Cloud STT**: S1-Voice and Ultra (Superwhisper's own), ElevenLabs Scribe V2, Deepgram Nova 2 / Nova 3 / Nova Medical.
- **Local LLMs for post-processing**: S1-mini (their own on-device model with tone control, shipped 2026-08-19), GPT OSS 20B, DeepSeek R1 Distill, Ministral 3 8B, Llama 3 8B, Mistral 7B v0.2, Llama 3.2 3B, Phi-2 3B.
- **Cloud LLMs**: Claude Sonnet 4.6 and 4.5, Claude Haiku 4.5, GPT-5 variants, Gemini 3 Flash and 3.1 Flash Lite, Grok 4.1 Fast, Llama 3.1 8B on Groq, S1-Language.
- **BYOK**: homepage advertises "personal API key integration" as a Pro feature; the models page says enterprise customers can swap in their own API keys. Slight conflict — treat BYOK as available but verify tier.
- **Vocabulary**: two distinct mechanisms, which is unusually thorough. *Vocabulary words* act as recognition hints biasing the speech model, explicitly documented as useful for proper nouns, acronyms and **multi-language words**, with a warning that too many entries can confuse the model and degrade punctuation and language detection. *Replacements* are programmatic post-transcription substitutions, case-insensitive matching with exact-case output. Docs recommend using vocabulary sparingly and leaning on replacements.
- **Streaming**: supported, shows words on screen as you speak, but **only with Deepgram Nova cloud models**. Enabled by switching to a Nova model in Advanced Settings then toggling Realtime. No local streaming.
- **Multilingual**: 100+ languages and dialects, translation to English available on S1-Voice, Ultra and most full Whisper local models but **not** on Parakeet or Deepgram. Parakeet V3 is multilingual and near-realtime with keyword recognition for up to 1,000 custom terms. No first-party claim of automatic German+English code-switching within one utterance.
- **Hotkeys**: toggle recording; push-to-talk press-and-hold where a quick tap acts as toggle; mouse-button binding with the same dual behaviour; dedicated cancel-recording key; press-and-hold to open a mode changer and cycle modes; and a separate per-mode shortcut that starts recording directly in that mode.
- **Modes architecture**: this is what makes it a platform rather than a dictation tool. Each mode bundles a speech model, a language model, a system prompt and actions. Built-in modes are Voice to Text, Message, Email, Note, Meeting and Super Mode; Pro users create unlimited custom modes. Mode switching happens via keyboard shortcut, menu bar, auto-activation per app, or deep links.
- **Privacy**: 100% offline operation when using local voice and language models. Documented guidance for sensitive healthcare/financial data steers users to local models. Account and data deletion docs exist. DPA and BAA available.
- **Distribution and activity**: direct download from superwhisper.com, requires macOS 13.3+. Not on the Mac App Store (iOS is). Sparkle updater **not confirmed by name**. Release cadence is aggressive: 2.17.3 Aug 17, 2.18.0 Aug 19, 2.18.1 Aug 20, 2.18.2 Aug 26, 2.18.3 Sep 3 2026. The 2.18.3 notes mention macOS 27 compatibility fixes.
- Sources: https://superwhisper.com/models · https://superwhisper.com/docs/billing/plans.md · https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/interface-vocabulary.md · https://superwhisper.com/docs/common-issues/realtime.md · https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/essential-settings.md · https://superwhisper.com/docs/models/voice.md · https://superwhisper.com/changelog · https://superwhisper.com/download · https://www.theglobeandmail.com/business/article-toronto-ai-startup-superwhisper-dictation-app/

### Wispr Flow — the funded cloud incumbent

- **Company**: Wispr AI Inc, San Francisco. Roughly 80 employees as of May 2026.
- **Funding**: **$280M Series B at a $2B valuation, announced Aug 17 2026**, led by Menlo Ventures, with Notable Capital, NEA, Neo Ventures, 8VC, MVP Ventures, Acrew, Forerunner, Goodwater, Peak XV, Together Fund and PLUS Capital. **Total raised $361M.** Previous round was $25M less than ten months earlier.
- **Traction**: iOS App Store 4.84 stars, **15,391 ratings**, v1.74, updated 2026-09-08. That is by far the largest verified rating base in this set. Third-party and secondary figures circulating: 2.5M downloads, 270 Fortune 500 customers, 125,000+ business customers, ~$10M ARR as of Oct 2025, 40% month-over-month growth, 70% twelve-month retention. **None of these are first-party verified** — TechCrunch explicitly notes ARR and user counts were not disclosed.
- **Pricing** (verified on their pricing page): Free $0 with 2,000 words/week on desktop, 1,000/week on iPhone, unlimited on Android, weekly meeting limit, speaker identification, MCP access to Claude/ChatGPT. Pro $15/user/month or $12/user/month annual, unlimited dictation, advanced AI models, early access, shared dictionary and snippets, team analytics. Growth $23 or $18 annual, adding SSO/SAML, HIPAA enforcement, admin controls, individual usage reporting. Enterprise custom, annual only, adding SCIM, audit logs, MDM, locked model training, SLAs. **No lifetime option.** Students get three months free and 50% off. 14-day Pro trial.
- **Local STT**: **none, at all.** Requires an active internet connection for everything. Does not work in airplane mode. The security FAQ confirms transcription is server-side and that "audio must be decrypted to produce a transcription", so there is no end-to-end encryption. This is the single largest strategic gap in the market leader.
- **Cloud providers**: not named. Subprocessors are listed only in Annex 2 of the DPA, available under NDA via their Trust Center.
- **Vocabulary**: Dictionary supports add/edit/delete/search of custom words and replacements with sorting and auto-correction rules, words capped at 30 characters. Snippets are saved text blocks triggered by voice, trigger capped at 60 characters, body at 4,000 characters. Rich-text formatting for snippets shipped Jul 29 2026.
- **Streaming**: no live partial text documented anywhere. Text pastes when you release the key.
- **Multilingual**: 100+ languages with automatic detection, plus a language picker so you can "switch between languages naturally". Notably their own FAQ **recommends selecting only your current language rather than relying on auto-detect** for best accuracy, which is an admission that code-switching costs accuracy. The Jun 24 2026 release notes mention improved accuracy routing specifically for UK English and **Swiss German**.
- **Hotkeys**: on Macs with an Apple fn key, **fn is the default**; without one it falls back to Ctrl+Opt. Hold to dictate and release to stop and paste. **Double-tap the hotkey to lock into hands-free mode**, from idle or mid-session. Fn+Space is a dedicated hands-free shortcut. Cmd+Ctrl+V pastes the last transcript. Esc cancels. No wake word or always-on listening.
- **Overlay**: the **Flow Bar**, a floating control at the bottom of the screen by default, hidden unless enabled in Settings. Draggable, snaps to screen edges, reorients vertically on a side edge. Clicking the centre bubble starts dictation, with Cancel and Stop while recording.
- **AI layer**: filler-word removal, mid-sentence self-correction, punctuation and formatting; Command Mode; Notetaker for bot-free meeting notes with speaker identification and Otter transcript import; MCP integration exposing dictation to Claude and ChatGPT; team leaderboards.
- **Privacy**: certifications are **SOC 2 Type I (April 2026, A-LIGN)** and **ISO 27001:2022 Stage 1 (April 2026)**, with HIPAA-aligned controls and BAA available, plus annual penetration testing. They explicitly do **not** hold SOC 2 Type II (observation pending), FedRAMP or PCI DSS. Note the marketing site claims "SOC 2 Type II, HIPAA and ISO 27001 certified", which **contradicts their own security FAQ** — flagging this as a genuine first-party inconsistency. Zero Data Retention requires Privacy Mode on *and* Dictation cloud storage off; **cloud storage is on by default**. HIPAA BAA customers get Privacy Mode enforced. Account with name, email and organisation is required.
- **Distribution and activity**: direct download for Mac and Windows desktop, **not on the Mac App Store**; iOS and Android via the stores. Latest desktop **v1.6.721, Sep 4 2026**. Cadence is roughly weekly.
- Sources: https://wisprflow.ai/ · https://wisprflow.ai/pricing · https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/3467817258-security-and-compliance-faq · https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/6391241694-use-flow-hands-free · https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/5096240724-navigating-the-wispr-flow-app-desktop-ios-and-android · https://wisprflow.ai/whats-new · https://wisprflow.ai/data-controls · https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/17/wispr-raises-280m-at-2b-valuation-as-it-looks-beyond-dictation/

### MacWhisper — the indie one-time-purchase transcription workhorse

- **Maker**: Jordi Bruin / Good Snooze.
- **Popularity, verified today**: the Gumroad page's own JSON-LD structured data gives **ratingValue 4.5 with reviewCount 2,572**. That is first-party and confirmed by direct HTTP fetch. **The sales count is not exposed** in the page HTML; it renders client-side. A secondary site claimed 223,515 sales, another claimed 519.4K sales in 30 days (obviously garbled). **Treat any specific sales number as unverified.**
- **Mac App Store**: listed as **"Whisper Transcription"**, not MacWhisper. Free with in-app purchases, **3.85 stars, 158 ratings**, version 5.0, updated **2026-09-11**, minimum macOS 18.0. IAP tiers observed: Pro $6.99 / $8.99 / $29.99, Pro Lifetime $89.99–$99.99, combined Pro & Assistant $12.99, Assistant subscription $9.99/mo or $89.99/yr. **Medium confidence on the exact IAP ladder.** Recent App Store release notes mention Siri and Shortcuts integration, Apple Intelligence search over transcripts, Spotlight indexing and Gemini BYOK for summaries.
- **Gumroad licensing**: free tier exists. Single Pro licence **€64 one-time with lifetime updates** per macwhisper.com today; older 2026 pages show €59, so the price appears to have risen during the year. Bulk Pro packs confirmed in the page JSON: 5-pack €54/licence, 10-pack €49, 20-pack €45, 50-pack €44.
- **Free vs Pro gating**: Free covers microphone transcription, MP3/WAV/M4A/OGG/OPUS/MOV/MP4, 100+ languages, SRT/VTT/TXT export, YouTube and media-URL transcription. Pro adds MD/PDF/HTML/DOCX export, speaker recognition, per-speaker podcast files, batch processing, transcript translation, **system-wide dictation with automatic grammar improvement**, AI dictation prompts, subtitle generation with inline video player, meeting recording for Zoom/Teams/Webex/Skype/Chime/Discord, and watched-folder automation.
- **Local engine**: **WhisperKit**, which is Apple-Silicon only, plus **NVIDIA Parakeet** (added around v13, mid-2025). **large-v3-turbo is available** and actively recommended in their docs. No first-party mention of MLX or Apple's Speech framework — unverified for MacWhisper.
- **Cloud and BYOK**: the broadest BYOK surface of any app here. OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI/Grok, Google Gemini, Ollama, DeepSeek, Azure, LM Studio, OpenRouter, Deepgram, ElevenLabs, Gladia, plus custom OpenAI-compatible endpoints. Changelog adds Cohere, Speechmatics and Gemini 3.5 Transcribe as cloud transcription providers.
- **System-wide dictation**: **only in the direct/Gumroad build, not the App Store build**, because of Apple sandboxing. Configurable global hotkey with a choice of **toggle or push-to-talk**. Double-tap and fn-specific gestures are **not** documented first-party. A floating **"Global overlay"** HUD sits on top by default, with auto-copy-to-clipboard and auto-start-recording options. Whether it auto-inserts at the cursor versus only copying is **not explicitly confirmed in the docs**.
- **Vocabulary**: "Global Replace" find-and-replace, Original→Replacement pairs applied to all new transcriptions, with whole-word-only and case-sensitivity toggles and JSON import/export. This is **post-hoc text replacement only**, not Whisper prompt biasing.
- **Streaming**: file transcription shows incremental results as it processes, but live dictation is not low-latency streaming. Third-party testing reports roughly 2.4 seconds before text appears and non-incremental delivery; consistent across sources but **not first-party verified**.
- **Multilingual**: 100 languages, plus a Translate mode to English for transcripts and subtitles. Docs state a hard limitation: **it cannot transcribe a single file containing multiple languages simultaneously**, and recommend running twice and merging manually. Bilingual Chinese-English handling is called out specifically.
- **Distribution**: direct DMG from macwhisper.com, **Homebrew cask** (`brew install --cask macwhisper`, formula at 14.8.1 requiring macOS 14+), Mac App Store as Whisper Transcription, plus iOS/iPadOS apps. Sparkle **likely but not confirmed by name**.
- **Activity**: confirmed history through v14.8.1 (Gemini 3.5 Transcribe), 14.8 (model download and dictation improvements), 14.7.1 (Cohere, Speechmatics), 14.7 (dictation keyboard language matching), 14.6 (editor). Roughly monthly minor releases with periodic majors; v11 shipped a redesigned transcript UI on Jul 14 2026. **Exact calendar date of v14.8.1 unverified.**

### Aiko — not actually a competitor

- **Maker**: Sindre Sorhus. Closed source, written in Swift/SwiftUI. No public repo for the app.
- **Price and traction**: **$24.00 one-time**, universal purchase across macOS, iOS and visionOS. **4.7 stars, 325 ratings.** Version 1.11.0, released **2026-06-27** per Apple's API, minimum macOS 26.0, 1.8 GB. 14-day trial via TestFlight. A third-party mirror claimed $22 earlier in 2026 and an Aug 11 2026 update date; **Apple's own API is authoritative, so use Jun 27 2026.**
- **Positioning**: this is a file and recording transcriber, **not a dictation tool**. It does **not** insert text at the cursor system-wide, and it explicitly does **not** do live transcription while recording. Sindre's own FAQ says live transcription is something he plans to look into but has deprioritised. You cannot use it during a live Zoom call, only on the recording afterwards.
- **Models**: on-device Whisper. **⚠️ Unresolved conflict**: the App Store listing says Whisper **large-v3** on Mac, while sindresorhus.com/aiko says **large v2**. iOS uses medium or small depending on device memory. Requires A12 Bionic or newer.
- **Vocabulary**: a word-replacement tool to auto-fix common mistakes. No prompt or vocabulary biasing.
- **Privacy**: strongest of the set by construction. All processing local, no data collection, no cloud upload, no account.
- **Languages**: 100+, quality varies. No code-switching claim.
- **Distribution**: App Store only, no direct DMG or Gumroad channel.
- Sources: https://sindresorhus.com/aiko · https://apps.apple.com/us/app/aiko/id1672085276

### Monologue — best documented mixed-language story

- **Maker**: Every Media Inc (every.to), the media company; Monologue sits alongside Cora, Spiral, Proof and Sparkle in the Every Bundle. **No funding or revenue figures found anywhere** — Every appears self-funded.
- **Traction**: iOS/iPadOS/watchOS app "Monologue: Smart Dictation" at **4.83 stars, 291 ratings**, v1.1.2, updated 2026-08-15. Site claims 500M+ words dictated, 100k+ hours saved, 100k+ notes captured. Launch post claims beta usage of 7,000 dictations/day and 1M words/week. A "4.9 / 172 ratings" figure in circulation traces to an SEO blog, not Apple.
- **Pricing**: Free trial is 1,000 words plus 10 recorded notes. Pro **$15/month or $144/year**. Early-bird was $10/month at launch. Also bundled into the Every Bundle at roughly $24–30/month — the pricing page and launch announcement disagree on the bundle price, **unresolved**. **No lifetime option.**
- **Local STT**: yes, on Apple Silicon Macs. Their docs say local mode "transcribes the raw audio on your Mac" with no network. **But the engine is never named** — no Whisper, Parakeet, CoreML or MLX mention in the official docs. One research pass surfaced filenames suggesting CoreML builds of NVIDIA Parakeet (`parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3-coreml`, `parakeet-ctc-110m-coreml`); **plausible but unverified.** Crucially, local mode is **partial**: "smart cleanup and context-aware formatting still use Monologue's online service", so the polished output still requires network. No local large-v3 claim.
- **Cloud providers**: the privacy policy names **Groq, Fireworks and OpenRouter**, each under contractual zero-retention. No BYOK.
- **Vocabulary**: auto-learned personal dictionary for names, acronyms and company vernacular, synced across Mac, iPhone, iPad and Watch along with custom modes.
- **Streaming**: not clearly documented as live partial text. An App Store changelog line for v1.1.2 mentions "improved real-time transcription delivery" — weak signal only.
- **Mixed language**: **the clearest first-party code-switching support in the entire commercial set.** Docs confirm you can select up to **three languages simultaneously**, or use Auto Detect, with explicit guidance: "if you switch languages in one sentence, include both languages in the selection." Marketing separately claims 100+ languages and switching to Spanish mid-sentence. This is directly relevant to a German+English use case.
- **Hotkeys**: push-to-talk is **hold right-Option, release to finish**, plus a hands-free toggle mode. iPhone uses a custom keyboard and the Action Button.
- **AI layer**: four modes (Chat, Email, Notes, Coding) plus custom instructions, per-app context awareness that adapts tone (casual in Slack, formal in email, technical in a code editor), optional **"deep context" screen reading** that reads surrounding code to format filenames and technical terms correctly, and an **MCP server giving AI agents read-only access to voice notes**. Also does bot-free meeting notes via screen-recording permission for system audio.
- **Privacy**: no server-side audio storage for dictation by default; local files auto-delete after 14 days with optional sync; zero-LLM-retention claimed via provider contracts. **Account required**, including Sign in with Apple.

### Apple macOS 26 (Tahoe) built-in Dictation — the free baseline

- **Cost**: free, ships with the OS. No account needed.
- **Activation**: the default is **press the fn (Globe) key twice**. On keyboards with a dedicated microphone key in the function row, a **single press** of the mic key does the same. Both are reconfigurable in System Settings → Keyboard → Dictation → Shortcut, and custom combinations such as Option-Z are possible. Also available via Edit → Start Dictation.
- **No push-to-talk.** Apple documents only press-and-release toggling. **Holding the fn key triggers Siri instead**, a different feature. There is no documented hold-to-talk Dictation gesture in macOS 26. This is a real gap a competitor can own.
- **System-wide insertion**: yes. Apple's wording is that you can "speak to enter text anywhere you can type it"; place the insertion point first and text lands at the cursor in any text field.
- **On-device processing**: **hybrid, not uniform.** The macOS Tahoe Feature Availability page carries a separate row for "Dictation: On-Device and Modeless Dictation" covering roughly **40 of the ~56 supported locales**, with the explicit note "Requires download of speech models." For locales outside that list, Dictation still works but processing is server-side. Apple's own Keyboard settings page links a disclosure about "when Dictation may send things you say and other information to Apple", confirming the split. So offline operation is guaranteed only for on-device locales after the model download.
- **"Enhanced Dictation"**: no longer exists as a named toggle or download in macOS 26 settings; that terminology was retired after Catalina. **Apple never explicitly announced the retirement**, so this is inferred from its absence in current docs.
- **Underlying framework**: macOS 26 introduces **SpeechAnalyzer** and **SpeechTranscriber** in the Speech framework, replacing the decade-old SFSpeechRecognizer. Apple states the new speech-to-text model "operates entirely on the device", is not charged against app memory or storage, is used by Notes, Voice Memos and Journal, handles long-form and distant audio, manages models via AssetInventory, supports **streaming with "volatile results"** followed by finalised results, and offers a `DictationTranscriber` fallback for locales the new model does not cover. **⚠️ Apple never states that the end-user Dictation feature itself runs on SpeechAnalyzer** — the API is framed as developer-facing, and the link is inferred, not confirmed.
- **Languages**: **56 language/locale variants** are listed for Dictation on the Tahoe feature availability page, spanning Arabic (Saudi Arabia and UAE), Cantonese (China and Hong Kong), multiple English, French, German, Spanish, Chinese, Japanese and Korean variants, through Vietnamese. Of those, roughly **30 support automatic punctuation** and roughly **40 support on-device/modeless dictation**.
- **Mixed language — the critical gap**: you can configure multiple Dictation languages, but **switching is entirely manual**. You either click the small language indicator that appears next to the text cursor while dictating, or press the **Globe key** and choose from the menu. **Apple documents no automatic mid-utterance code-switching.** There is no way to speak a German sentence containing English technical terms and have both handled in one pass. The SpeechAnalyzer API reinforces this: `SpeechTranscriber(locale:)` is scoped to one locale per session, so Apple's speech stack is single-locale-per-session by design. This is explicitly a documented absence, not a research gap.
- **Custom vocabulary — the second critical gap**: **there is no user-defined custom vocabulary or dictionary for Dictation.** A 1,000-term **Vocabulary** feature with custom pronunciations, importable and exportable per language, exists under System Settings → Accessibility → **Voice Control** — a different feature, and Apple's docs note that **enabling Voice Control disables standard Dictation**, so the two are mutually exclusive and you cannot stack them. Whether the separate **Text Replacements** feature in Keyboard settings affects dictated text is **not documented by Apple either way** — unverified, treat with caution.
- **Streaming**: the system shows live interim text near the cursor and finalises on pause, but Apple's consumer-facing docs never use the words "real-time streaming" for the end-user feature. The developer API is explicitly streaming-capable. **Word-by-word live rendering for the end-user feature is strongly implied but not Apple-documented.**
- **Punctuation and limits**: automatic punctuation inserts commas, periods and question marks in supported languages and is toggleable. **No length limit** — "you can dictate text of any length without a timeout" — but Dictation **stops automatically after 30 seconds of silence**. That silence-timeout wording is identical in the macOS 15 docs, so it is not new to 26.
- **What is new in 26 versus 15**: the only confirmed first-party change is the SpeechAnalyzer/SpeechTranscriber API family. **Apple's own macOS Tahoe 26 newsroom press release does not mention Dictation, SpeechAnalyzer or SpeechTranscriber at all**; the only speech-adjacent headline is Live Translation in Phone, Messages and FaceTime. Third-party blogs claim macOS 26 Dictation added name-spelling support, expanded voice text-editing commands and a menu-bar waveform indicator; **none of that is confirmed by any Apple source** and should be treated as unverified.
- Sources: https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/use-dictation-mh40584/26/mac/26 · https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/keyboard-settings-kbdm162/26/mac/26 · https://www.apple.com/macos/feature-availability/ · https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/create-import-export-a-custom-vocabulary-mchl3eb7b79a/mac · https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/commands-for-dictating-text-on-mac-mh40695/26/mac/26 · https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/277/ · https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/06/macos-tahoe-26-makes-the-mac-more-capable-productive-and-intelligent-than-ever/

## 3. Cross-cutting observations

1. **Local STT is the fault line.** Wispr Flow, Typeless and Voicy have no local mode at all. Willow's is claimed but disputed. Monologue's is partial, since cleanup and formatting still hit the network. Only **Superwhisper and MacWhisper** ship a genuinely complete local pipeline with a named engine and large-v3-class models, and only Superwhisper also runs the LLM cleanup locally via S1-mini.
2. **Nobody streams properly.** Superwhisper streams only via Deepgram's cloud Nova models. Everyone else either says nothing or makes a latency claim without documenting live partials. True local streaming transcription is unclaimed territory.
3. **BYOK is almost absent.** Only MacWhisper offers broad bring-your-own-key across 14+ providers. Superwhisper offers it on Pro or Enterprise depending on which page you read. The other seven commercial apps offer none.
4. **Nobody names their cloud STT vendor** except Monologue, which names Groq, Fireworks and OpenRouter as LLM infrastructure rather than necessarily STT.
5. **Mixed-language code-switching is barely addressed.** Only **Monologue** documents it concretely, with up to three simultaneous languages and explicit mid-sentence guidance. Whisper Flow claims automatic in-dictation switching. Wispr Flow's own FAQ advises *against* relying on auto-detect. MacWhisper's docs state the limitation outright. Apple does not support it. For a German plus English code-switching user, the entire commercial field is weak.
6. **Custom vocabulary is nearly universal but mostly shallow.** Every paid app except Apple has one. Only **Superwhisper** distinguishes model-biasing hints from post-hoc replacements; MacWhisper and Aiko offer post-hoc replacement only.
7. **Hotkey sophistication clusters at the top.** Willow Voice and Wispr Flow both do fn-hold plus double-tap-for-hands-free. Superwhisper adds mouse-button binding and hold-to-switch-mode. Apple offers neither push-to-talk nor double-tap-for-hands-free.
8. **Price structure splits cleanly.** One-time purchase: MacWhisper €64, Aiko $24, Superwhisper $249.99 lifetime, Voicy $260 lifetime. Subscription-only: Wispr Flow, Monologue, Typeless, Willow, Whisper Flow. The subscription apps converge tightly on **$15/month or $12/month annual**, which is now effectively the market's anchor price.
9. **The Mac App Store is not where this market lives.** Only Aiko, MacWhisper's cut-down "Whisper Transcription" build and Voicely Type are on it. Superwhisper, Wispr Flow, Monologue, Typeless, Willow and Voicy all ship the Mac app by direct download, because system-wide dictation is incompatible with App Store sandboxing (MacWhisper documents this explicitly as the reason its store build omits dictation).
10. **Apple's baseline leaves three exploitable gaps**: no push-to-talk, no custom vocabulary for Dictation, and no automatic mixed-language code-switching. All three are things a third-party app can own outright.
